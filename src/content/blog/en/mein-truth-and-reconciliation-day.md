September 30 was the National Day for Truth and Reconciliation in Canada. It is a day to remember the children who were forced to attend residential schools. These boarding schools were designed to separate Indigenous children from their families, languages and cultures. The day remembers the children who never came home and honours the survivors, their families and communities.

September 30 is also Orange Shirt Day. The colour comes from Phyllis Webstad’s story. She was six when the new orange shirt she had picked out was taken from her on her first day at residential school. Today, many people wear orange on September 30 to remember her experience and those of the other children. [Phyllis Webstad tells her story at the Orange Shirt Society.](https://orangeshirtday.org/about/phyllis-story/)

## Wearing orange to Grandview Park

I wanted to see how people in Vancouver marked the day, so I asked my German roommate if she wanted to come along to the Honour Walk. We met up with everyone outside the Vancouver Aboriginal Community Policing Centre on Franklin Street. There were already plenty of people in orange, and while we waited for the walk to start, two dogs beside us made friends. One of them was even wearing an orange bandana.

![A black dog in an orange bandana and a light-coloured dog face each other on the sidewalk](<../../../assets/photos/mein-truth-and-reconciliation-day/WhatsApp Image 2026-11-14 at 16.26.08 (1).jpeg>)

_These two found each other at the meeting point, too. My own photo._

I’d thought of all sorts of things while packing for Canada. Apparently, something orange hadn’t crossed my mind. Luckily, my roommate lent me an orange knit sweater, which I tied around my waist. You can see my improvised solution in the photo of the two of us.

![My roommate and I take a selfie wearing orange, with other people behind us](<../../../assets/photos/mein-truth-and-reconciliation-day/WhatsApp Image 2026-11-14 at 16.26.09.jpeg>)

_Our selfie with the borrowed sweater. My own photo._

Then the group set off towards Grandview Park. As we crossed East Hastings Street, there was still orange everywhere I looked ahead. Once we got moving, I could really see how many people had come out to walk together that morning.

![Honour Walk participants walk along a paved street under a partly cloudy sky](<../../../assets/photos/mein-truth-and-reconciliation-day/WhatsApp Image 2026-11-14 at 16.26.09 (1).jpeg>)

_On the Honour Walk in East Vancouver. My own photo._

The group carried on towards Grandview Park while we took a little detour into a Mexican shop. We had a look around and found a few samples to try. What am I holding in that photo? No idea anymore. I just remember it tasting really good. Good enough to take a picture, anyway.

![A hand holding a small wrapped sample](<../../../assets/photos/mein-truth-and-reconciliation-day/WhatsApp Image 2026-11-14 at 16.26.09 (3).jpeg>)

_I kept the photo. I wish I’d remembered the name. My own photo._

Everyone came together again at Grandview Park. There were tents and market stalls on the grass, so we had a look around. The masks and all the different pieces of clothing are what I remember most.

![People dressed in orange gather on a lawn with trees and market tents in the background](<../../../assets/photos/mein-truth-and-reconciliation-day/WhatsApp Image 2026-11-14 at 16.26.09 (2).jpeg>)

_The gathering in Grandview Park after the walk. My own photo._

## A stop in Gastown

I’d been meaning to spend some time in Gastown anyway, so we headed there after the gathering. We wandered past little shops and old brick buildings. I liked the neighbourhood straight away, especially the streets lined with trees.

![A tree-lined street with brick buildings in Gastown](<../../../assets/photos/mein-truth-and-reconciliation-day/WhatsApp Image 2026-11-14 at 16.35.25.jpeg>)

_Walking through Gastown. My own photo._

A little further along, I spotted the Vancouver Lookout across the street. Between the buildings, we could see the round observation deck on top of Harbour Centre.

![The round Vancouver Lookout observation deck rises above buildings and trees](<../../../assets/photos/mein-truth-and-reconciliation-day/WhatsApp Image 2026-11-14 at 16.35.25 (1).jpeg>)

_The Vancouver Lookout between the buildings. My own photo._

Soon after, we came across another highlight, the Gastown Steam Clock on Water Street. Every fifteen minutes it sounds its steam whistles. A steaming clock in the middle of the sidewalk was something I hadn’t expected to find in Vancouver. I can’t quite say why, but I found it pretty charming.

![Backlit steam rises from the Gastown Steam Clock](<../../../assets/photos/mein-truth-and-reconciliation-day/WhatsApp Image 2026-11-14 at 16.35.25 (2).jpeg>)

_The Steam Clock on Water Street. My own photo._

To finish the day, we went down to the waterfront. From there we could see the white sails of Canada Place, where the cruise ships dock, and the mountains across the water. I liked getting to see so many different sides of Vancouver in one day. The moment that stayed with me most was from that morning, when the whole group in orange set off together. I was glad we could join the walk and take part in remembering together.

![Vancouver harbour with the white sails of Canada Place on the left and mountains on the horizon](<../../../assets/photos/mein-truth-and-reconciliation-day/WhatsApp Image 2026-11-14 at 16.35.25 (3).jpeg>)

_One last look at the water that day. My own photo._
